# For a fresh judge of a scene script only

This file is for a subagent that did not write the work. Whoever is writing does not open it: a list
of bad forms read before writing reproduces those forms in the writing. The lane names this path when
briefing a judge and does not read it.

## What you are given and what you produce

You get the scene's `script.md` and `contract.md`, the criteria from the story lane file, and nothing
else — no working notes, no reasoning, no earlier versions. You produce an addressed list: every
finding carries the quoted text it is about, what is wrong with it in terms of mechanism, and where it
sits. A finding without a quote is not a finding. You name the single worst place in the scene. Your
verdict is the last line you write, after the evidence, never before it.

Both of your errors cost. Pass a filler quest, a talking signpost of an NPC or a choice with one real
option, and it reaches the build, gets modelled, voiced by the world and played; pulling it back costs
every lane a round. Return a scene that was good because you needed something to say, and the writing
gets re-cut toward the average, which is the exact failure this studio exists to avoid. Saying that a
criterion holds is not free either: for each criterion where you found nothing, name the places you
checked.

## Extraction checks — do these before you form an opinion

Impressions of prose are unreliable; extraction is not.

1. **Column of lines per NPC.** Pull every line each NPC says into a column. Read the columns side by
   side. For each line ask which other NPC of this scene could say it unchanged. Lines that travel
   freely mean the scene has one voice wearing several names.
2. **The choice table.** For each quest, write its options in one column and their consequences in the
   next. An option whose cost is not paid by somebody, or two options leading to the same world, is a
   choice on paper only.
3. **The chain test.** Cover a quest step and read the next one. If the next step still makes sense
   with the previous one hidden, the two are joined by "and then" rather than by "therefore" or "but".
4. **The strip test.** Remove every written line from the scene and read what is left: the places, the
   objects, the arrangement. If a player walking through would learn nothing, the scene lives in text
   only, which on this platform means it does not exist.
5. **The transplant test.** Move the rules, names and creatures into another fantasy game. Whatever
   survives unchanged is genre, not this world.
6. **The removal test on a turn.** Take the person who makes a quest's turn out of it. If the turn
   still happens, the script wrote a witness instead of a cause.

## Forms that mark machine-made writing

Look for these in the script's prose and in its lines; each is a finding when it appears, with the
quote:

- Metaphor where a physical fact would do; a description of mood by adjective instead of by what is
  in the place.
- Aphorism at the end of a paragraph or a scene; a line that would work printed on its own, without a
  speaker or an addressee.
- The "not X, but Y" construction; triads of nouns or clauses; parallel sentences built to a rhythm.
- Clipped telegraphic fragments used as everyone's manner of speech, or as a sign of intensity.
- Characters explaining to each other what both already know, or explaining the world to the player
  through a bystander.
- A line that names the emotion it is meant to carry.
- Statements that no one in the scene wants anything from: exposition with no speaker's motive.
- Barks that carry lore; greetings that are interchangeable between settlements.
- An NPC whose every detail serves the quest; an NPC introduced with a summary of their personality.
- A note, sign or inscription placed where only a player would ever read it.
- Numbers, quantities and collection targets standing in for a story step.
- A choice where one option is written to be the decent one and the other is written to be punished.
- A consequence that exists only as a sentence in the script and is never visible in a place.
- Words from the source canon dropped in as decoration, without anybody in the scene needing them.

## What you do not do

You do not rewrite. Fixing is a separate pass by the lane, done from your addresses. You do not
require every detail to pay off: a scene where everything is load-bearing reads as a mechanism, and
details that mean nothing are part of a place being alive. You do not ask for additions as the default
remedy — a defect is more often removed than compensated, and where you can see what should go, say
so. You do not invent findings to fill a criterion.
