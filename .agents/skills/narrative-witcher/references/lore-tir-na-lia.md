# Lore reference: the world of the Alders, its city, and Ciri after the war

Every entry is marked: **[book]** canon of the novels, **[W3]** canon of The Witcher 3, **[W4]**
confirmed by CDPR for The Witcher 4, **[rumour]** unconfirmed reading, **[ours]** our invention, to be
written into `game/LORE.md` and marked there. State nothing about this world in a script that is not
[book], [W3], [W4] or an [ours] entry already recorded in the lore bible.

Russian equivalents of the canonical names are given in brackets where the owner is likely to use
them; the game's own text is written in English (the story lane file states the rule).

## The Aen Elle and their world

- **Aen Elle** [Aen Elle, «Народ Ольх»], the People of the Alders: elves of another world, culturally
  apart from the elves of the Continent. Both peoples left a dying ancestral world before the
  cataclysm that mixed the spheres. [book/W3]
- In their new world they found unicorns and went to war with them over the ability to move between
  worlds; with the Wild Hunt they raided other worlds and took humans as slaves. Mountains of human
  bones have been found on their world. [book]
- Physically: tall, long-lived by centuries, speaking a variant of the Elder Speech. [book/W3]
- **Ard Gaeth**, the Gate of the Worlds, is lost; since then they depend on navigators — mages who
  open limited portals. That loss forced small raids and a strategy of terror: they amplified their
  spectral projections until they were remembered not as soldiers from another world but as doom
  itself, with winter as the mask of the Hunt. [book/W3]
- To the people of the Continent they are wraiths, an omen of war and plague; returned captives speak
  of another world, strange elves, unicorns, distorted time and years lost in what felt like days,
  and are not believed. [book/W3]
- In the games' continuity their world is dying of the **White Frost** [Белый Хлад]; their mages can
  study and slow it, not stop it. The Hunt is not redeemed by the Frost: it is radicalised by it. In
  the city itself a report on the advance of the Frost lies on a bench by a fountain. [W3]

## Tir ná Lia [Тир на Лиа]

- The capital of the Aen Elle world, seat of king **Auberon Muircetach**; the river **Easnadh** runs
  through it. [book]
- Architecture as the novels describe it: buildings light and open on all sides, so airy and delicate
  that they look less like buildings than like phantoms of buildings; marble, alabaster and malachite;
  stucco, terracotta and mosaic; many bridges and terraces, peristyles, cloisters, balconies,
  sculptures and monuments. [book]
- The game showed the city once and built it as an ordinary medieval town instead, with the Palace of
  Awakening as the viceroy's residence and a district called Tuathe; the place holds almost nothing to
  pick up. [W3] For a stylised Roblox world the novels' image is the stronger and the more
  recognisable of the two; which one this game uses is an [ours] decision that belongs in the lore
  bible.
- Other peoples know this world under the names of the otherworld of Celtic legend. [book]
- **Ge'els**: viceroy, painter, whose portraiture had already left medieval convention behind; loyal
  to Auberon, without ambition for the throne. [W3]
- Social types the source material fixes: aristocrats, slave traders, navigators, craftsmen in marble
  and mosaic, human servants descended from captives, and the veterans of the Hunt's cavalry. [W3,
  and [ours] where extended]

## Ciri in that world [book]

- She arrives through a portal; time does not work as it does at home, and there is no obvious way
  back.
- She meets **Avallac'h** and **Eredin**, commander of the Hunt's riders. The unicorns become restless
  the moment she enters their world.
- She is required to bear a child to Auberon, who is cold and dependent on a drug of that world.
  Eredin gives her a phial and hints at poison; Auberon dies of an overdose, and Eredin takes the
  throne by it. [book; the game confirms Eredin's hand through a seer's dream, W3]
- On her mare **Kelpie** she tries to break out; the unicorns surround her meaning to kill her, fearing
  her power, and **Ihuarraquax**, whom she once healed as a foal in a desert, saves her. The unicorns
  show her that the barriers can only be passed by boat. She wounds Eredin and leaves between worlds
  with Ihuarraquax's help.
- After that world she says that hatred and vengeance had blinded her and that she will stand before
  them in humility — the clearest statement of her state after these events. [book]
- At the end of the saga Ihuarraquax, through her, heals the two people she loves most, and she sends
  Kelpie back to the unicorns' world with him. In the announced next game Kelpie is with her again; the
  studio has not reconciled the two. [book vs W4]

## The Hunt after the war [W3]

- Imlerith is killed on the bald mountain; Caranthir, the navigator, and Eredin are killed at the
  ship. Ge'els is the only one of Eredin's three generals to survive, and the game does not show what
  becomes of him.
- Ge'els held back the reinforcements from Tir ná Lia in the final battle, which is part of why the
  Hunt lost. The world is left with no king, a painter for a viceroy, no Gate, and the Frost coming.
- Avallac'h's fate depends on the ending taken.
- Everything about who now rules, how far the Frost has come and what the veterans of the Hunt do with
  themselves is [ours] to decide and to record.

## Elder Blood and the unicorns

- **Elder Blood** / Hen Ichaer: the result of elven selective breeding, meant to open the gates
  between worlds without unicorns; Lara Dorren carried it, Ciri descends from her. [book]
- Unicorns are sentient multidimensional beings who cross the multiverse and observe less advanced
  species; they speak by telepathy in confirmations and negations, call other peoples simply beings,
  and despise those who use Power. They hate the Aen Elle for forcing them to open gates. They live in
  herds with a council of elders; the horn is an antidote and a detector of poison and changes colour
  with age. [book/W3]
- **Ihuarraquax**, "Little Horse": saved by Ciri as a foal when she used fire she had been forbidden
  to use; later he saves her from Eredin and heals her family. [book]
- The legend that a unicorn is drawn to a maiden is mocked inside the books themselves. The tone to
  copy is exactly that: the myth, and a local's contempt for the myth, in the same breath. [book]

## Ciri after the war and what is known of the next game

- The endings in which she survives are the canon the next game builds on; the ending where she dies
  is incompatible with it. [W4]
- She is a witcher there, having gone through the mutation; who performed it, how and at what cost is
  deliberately unknown, and the studio says that story is the next game's to tell. In the novels she
  never becomes a witcher. [W4 vs book]
- She is around twenty-five. A widely circulated quote from the author about the mutation of women is
  a fabrication. [W4/rumour]
- The next game is set in the wealthy northern kingdoms, not in the elven world: our location does not
  collide with it and must not pre-empt it. [W4]
- Her kit there: steel and silver swords, potions, a chain, water-born magic and the witcher signs;
  her manner is passionate and personally involved where the older protagonist kept practised
  distance. [W4]
- How the studio handled her with limited screen time is the model for us: they picked two focuses —
  her relationship with the man who raised her, and her ability to walk between worlds — and let
  everything else be alluded to in single lines. [W3]

## Bestiary and rites worth using

- The creature born of an improperly buried stillbirth, which hunts pregnant women and is laid to rest
  by a naming and burial rite rather than killed: Slavic in origin, and the model of theme-as-monster.
  [W3/folklore]
- The noon spirit that appears in fields at the height of the sun, announced by grain swaying on a
  windless day, born of heat, grief and the sweat of ploughmen. [W3/folklore]
- The two-hearted, two-souled undead woman: in the books a curse, in the folklore a birth; a
  seventeenth-century burial with a sickle at the throat has been found in Poland. [folklore]
- The lord of the forest whom shepherds bargained with — bread, salt and eggs at a crossroads for the
  safety of the flock — who punished greed rather than trespass. [folklore]
- The house spirit at the spinning wheel, which the source game turned into a swamp insect: the folk
  version is the richer one. [folklore]
- Days of the dead: a real Slavic feast of remembrance underlies one of the studio's strongest
  expansions. [folklore/W3]
