# Vision — the owner's voice

This file is written by the owner, once, and is the only place the owner speaks to the studio. The
lead may add dated notes under "Lead annotations"; nobody rewrites the sections above it. Every lane
reads this file before its first task of a scene. Leave a field empty rather than guessing.

## The game in one paragraph
A single-player open-world action adventure on Roblox set after The Witcher 3: the player is Ciri,
returning to Tir ná Lia, the city of the Aen Elle, years after the Wild Hunt fell. The city is half
alive and half frozen by what the White Frost left behind; the player crosses it from the waterfront
to the palace, fighting the remnants of the Hunt and the things the Frost made, meeting the elves and
humans who still live there, and choosing what to do about them. An evening in it should feel like a
Witcher chapter: a place with a history, people with their own business, a hunt with preparation and
consequence, and a fight that rewards precision and speed.

## Fantasy and tone
The Witcher: moral grey, grounded people, dry humour, folklore monsters with a mechanism, places that
carry the traces of what happened in them. Take the atmosphere and the quest craft, not the
photorealism. Style is stylized realism as Roblox's best studios ship it (contract §9), anchored by
the studio's own target frames: pale stone, crimson autumn, warm lanterns against cool dusk, mist.
Avoid: generic fantasy, cartoon violence, text walls, anything that reads as a template or a scan.

## Player and session
Teens and adults who know the Witcher or love action RPGs; keyboard first, gamepad supported, touch
not a target for the first scenes; solo; sessions of twenty to forty minutes. The first ten minutes
must deliver: the waterfront frame, the feel of blink, one fight that teaches by doing, and one
person worth talking to.

## Pillars (three, in order)
1. Speed and fragility: Ciri moves on constant blinks and dies fast when caught; a lane can tell
   this is honoured when standing still is never the best option and every hit taken is readable.
2. Every enemy is a different problem: no standard swordsmen; each enemy's mechanism punishes a
   habit and rewards a precise blink or chain; honoured when two enemies in a room change how the
   player moves.
3. A world that lives without the player: NPCs with their own schedules and lines, places that tell
   what happened in them, quests with a choice and a consequence; honoured when a capture with the
   character removed still reads as a place where people live.

## World
Tir ná Lia and its waters, post-W3: the elven waterfront and pier, the frozen harbour with the
Naglfar, the terraces and gardens, the palace gate, the lower quarters. Landmarks: the palace spire
(lit or dark tells the state of the city), the great gate, the Naglfar in the ice, the waterfall.
Factions: the remaining Aen Elle (under Ge'els), the Hunt's remnants, the humans who were kept as
servants, and the Frost itself.

## Scenes (first list; story refines it in game/PLAN.md)
1. pier — the elven waterfront at dusk: arrival, first blink, first hounds, the first person to talk to
2. harbour — the frozen harbour and the deck of the Naglfar: the Hunt's warriors, the captain, the ship breaking free

## Hard limits
No content beyond a teen rating (no gore, no sexual content, no real-world hate). No monetisation
in the first scenes. No use of the Witcher name, character names or marks in anything published;
the in-game names are the studio's own (the lore skill lists the substitutes). No real-world
persons.

## Permissions
- Upload to Roblox (asset upload, publish to a place): yes, uploads to the owner's account for the open Studio place; publishing the place publicly: no
- Spend money (paid APIs, marketplaces): no, except the keys already set in the environment
- Use external asset sources beyond assets/library/: yes, licences allowed CC0, CC-BY, MIT, Roblox Creator Store terms

## How I will judge a scene
The side-by-side of the target frame and the Studio capture first; then a play clip of a fight;
then whether the world reads as inhabited with the character removed.
